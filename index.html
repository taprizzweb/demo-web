The story of our neighbour

The Eiffel Tower, la Dame de Fer.

In 1889, Gustave Eiffel's company raised a 300-metre iron tower on the Champ de Mars as the entrance arch to the Exposition Universelle, marking 100 years since the French Revolution. Many Parisian artists called it an eyesore and it was only meant to stand for 20 years. It survived thanks to science: its height made it a perfect radio antenna.

Today it is the symbol of Paris, and after dark it puts on a show: for five minutes at the start of every hour, twenty thousand bulbs make the whole tower sparkle. From our picnic blankets on the Champ de Mars, you have a front-row seat.
